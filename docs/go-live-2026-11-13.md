From about 16:00 UTC on 13 November 2026 the new platform answers at
`https://doinggoodleeds.org.uk`. It was `partnership.doinggoodleeds.org.uk`
before. The old site keeps running on its Wordify address for the team.

## Who did what

- **Martin:** removed the domain from the old site in Wordify, made
  `doinggoodleeds.org.uk` the new site's domain there (WordPress `home` is now

  opening the site to search engines (below).

Mail records (MX, SPF, DKIM, Brevo, SMTP2GO) were not touched.

## Search engines

"Discourage search engines" was still ticked after the domain moved, so the
main domain was serving `noindex` on every page. It is now unticked
(`blog_public` 1) and the cache was cleared. Checked from outside: the home
page and `/support/trustees/` carry no `noindex`, canonicals use
`https://doinggoodleeds.org.uk`, and `/dgl-sitemap.xml` answers with 10
addresses. The sitemaps were submitted the same
afternoon; see "Sitemaps" below.

## Sitemaps

The site has two sitemaps, both listed in `robots.txt`:

- `/wp-sitemap.xml`, WordPress's own: pages, news, events, training and two
  topic lists. It only exists while search engines are allowed, so it was a
  404 until "Discourage search engines" was unticked.
- `/dgl-sitemap.xml`, the platform's: directory, organisation pages,
  calendar and listing pages (10 addresses today).

From the evening of 13 November Rank Math is on and supplies the main
sitemap, `/sitemap_index.xml` (pages, news, events, training, topics);
`/wp-sitemap.xml` now redirects to it. Nothing here is Astro; this site is
WordPress.

Both were submitted in Google Search Console on 13 November, in the
`https://doinggoodleeds.org.uk/` property (Martin's RYCM Google account).
Google read both the same day: status Success, 10 pages found in
`/dgl-sitemap.xml`; `/wp-sitemap.xml` is an index and showed 0 until its
six parts are read. The old site's `/sitemap.xml` (submitted 2020, 5,444
pages) is still listed there and now points at nothing; it was left in
place. There is no access to a domain-wide property
(`sc-domain:doinggoodleeds.org.uk`) on that account.

**IndexNow (Bing and others):** done once Rank Math was running. Rank Math
generated its own key and serves it at `/<key>.txt`. 72 addresses (the
pages, the lists, four organisation pages, and the events and training
items in the sitemap) were posted to `api.indexnow.org` and accepted (HTTP
202). Rank Math's Instant Indexing is set to send pages, news, events,
training and venues from now on.

## Rank Math: now on, after three plugin fixes

**What was found.** Rank Math and Rank Math Pro were active but had never
been registered or set up, so they did nothing: no meta description, no
social tags, no sitemap, WordPress's own titles.

**First attempt (about 16:35 to 16:39 UTC): the platform's pages went
down.** Claude ran the setup wizard. Ordinary pages were fine, but every
page the platform owns answered **HTTP 500**: `/news/`, `/events/`,
`/training/`, `/directory/`, `/directory/forum-central/`, `/spaces/`. Both
Rank Math plugins were deactivated within a few minutes and the pages
confirmed loading again.

**Cause, reproduced on a local WordPress.** `Seo::page()` asked WordPress
for the document title while describing the page; Rank Math answered that
and asked the plugin's `rank_math/frontend/title` bridge; the bridge called
`Seo::page()` again, without end, until PHP ran out of memory.

**The fixes, all released and deployed on 13 November:**

| Release | What it fixed |
|---|---|
| 0.59.2 | The loop above. Also the plugin's schema printing twice with Rank Math on, and the home page search box turning black-on-navy when focused |
| 0.59.3 | With Rank Math on, `/dashboard/` took Rank Math's `index, follow` and the news list's title and canonical. The noindex header and robots.txt block stayed in place throughout. Also one focus ring on the search box instead of two |
| 0.59.4 | `/dgl-sitemap.xml` answered 404 with Rank Math on, because Rank Math's rule claims any address ending `-sitemap.xml` |

Between 0.59.2 and 0.59.3 Rank Math was on for about two minutes and then
switched off again when the member-area problem showed.

**State now.** Rank Math and Rank Math Pro are **active** on production
with plugin 0.59.4. Checked on the live site with both on: the five lists,
an organisation page, the calendar, one news story, one event, one training
course, the home page, two ordinary pages and two member-area pages all
answer 200, each with one description, one canonical and one sharing title;
the member area prints `noindex, nofollow`, no canonical and no sharing
tags, with its own titles. The same checks were run again after 0.59.4 went out,
when `/dgl-sitemap.xml` answered 200 with 10 addresses, `/sitemap_index.xml`
200 with 8 parts, and `/wp-sitemap.xml` redirected to it. Rank Math Pro
updated itself from 3.0.88 to 3.0.125 when it was switched on.

**Settings chosen:** organisation "Doing Good Leeds" (was a person,
"DGL.org.uk"); page titles "%title% - Doing Good Leeds"; the home page's own
title "Doing Good Leeds - Support for the third sector in Leeds" and a
description taken from its intro; author archives off; sitemap includes
news, events, training, spaces and topics and leaves out Blocksy content
blocks and authors; external links not forced into a new tab; the modules
for WooCommerce, BuddyPress, bbPress, ACF and Web Stories off. Martin
connected his Rank Math account. Not done: a default sharing image (1200 x
630), the old Yoast data the wizard offered to import, and Rank Math's own
Redirections and 404 Monitor modules (the old-site redirects are in
Cloudflare).

**If a page the platform owns ever answers 500 again:** deactivate both
Rank Math plugins first (`wp plugin deactivate seo-by-rank-math-pro
seo-by-rank-math`), then look.

### Links switched to HTTPS

Each linked site was requested over HTTPS first. 33 hosts answered with a
valid certificate and were switched with one `wp search-replace` on
`wp_posts.post_content`, limited to those host names (130 rows, which
includes the stories' stored revisions). Four of them (lcf.co.uk,
ticketsource.com, bit.ly, facebook.com) refuse automated requests with a 403
or 400 on both http and https, so only their certificate was confirmed.

Two were left on `http://` because HTTPS fails there and the link would
break:

| Story | Link | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 6270, Remembering What's Forgotten Digital Exhibition | `http://www.rememberingwhatsforgotten.co.uk` | HTTPS answers 526, invalid certificate at the origin |
| 5339, Volunteer Drivers Needed | `http://phab-leeds.org.uk` | certificate fails verification |

Story 5327 had an email address pasted as a web link
(`http://kaye.wilson@val.org.uk/`, split across two links). It is now one
`mailto:kaye.wilson@val.org.uk` link.

The audit re-run afterwards lists only the two rows above. These edits went
through `wp search-replace`, so they did not write the plugin's own audit
row or a new revision.

## A warning for next time

Loading about 300 pages from one machine in an hour to take screenshots and
test links got that machine's IP address refused by the host: first
connection timeouts, then 403 on roughly half of all requests, while the
site answered normally from elsewhere. Anyone on the same office connection
would have seen the same errors. Test in smaller batches, or from outside.
